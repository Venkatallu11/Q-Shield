## Before you believe any of this

**55% of the values behind these numbers were not observed.** They were filled with placeholders so the model could run:

- `business_criticality`
- `data_lifetime_years`
- `exposure`
- `migration_cost`
- `rotation_hours`
- `sensitivity`
- `verification_horizon_years`

Nothing in a certificate records them. Run `qshield robustness` — already summarised below if present — to find out whether the recommendation actually depends on them.

> sensitivity, exposure, data_lifetime_years, business_criticality, migration_cost and rotation_hours are not recorded in X.509 and were filled with placeholders; run `qshield robustness` before acting on any number that depends on them

> 1 certificate(s) have already expired and are scored as deployed anyway: legacy.example.test

> no dependency edges: a certificate inventory records trust relations but not which service reaches which, so the attack-path term of the objective is inert and only node risk and trust inheritance are scored. Supply --entrypoints/--targets and dependency edges once the service topology is known

This is a scenario model. Its quantum factors and objective weights are uncalibrated hypotheses, not measurements, and a lower score does not mean a system is safe. It models quantum risk only — not phishing, credential theft, misconfiguration or key mismanagement, which are the likelier ways any of these assets is actually compromised.
